A. Transformation of plane stress

Sign convention (Beer and Johnston). Tension positive; τxy\tau_{xy} positive when it acts on a positive face in the positive direction of an axis (or on a negative face in the negative direction), with τxy=τyx\tau_{xy}=\tau_{yx}; θ\theta positive counter-clockwise from the xx axis to the x′x' axis. Plane stress: σz=τxz=τyz=0\sigma_z=\tau_{xz}=\tau_{yz}=0.

Transformation equations

σx′\sigma_{x'}​=σx+σy2+σx−σy2cos⁡2θ+τxysin⁡2θ=\dfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}+\dfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\cos2\theta+\tau_{xy}\sin2\theta

τx′y′\tau_{x'y'}​=−σx−σy2sin⁡2θ+τxycos⁡2θ=-\dfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\sin2\theta+\tau_{xy}\cos2\theta

σy′\sigma_{y'}​=σx+σy2−σx−σy2cos⁡2θ−τxysin⁡2θ=\dfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}-\dfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\cos2\theta-\tau_{xy}\sin2\theta

Invariant (check)

M1. Stresses on a given plane (analytical)

Use when: σx,σy,τxy\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\tau_{xy} are known and the stresses on one rotated plane are wanted.

  1. Draw the element and mark σx\sigma_x, σy\sigma_y, τxy\tau_{xy} with the sign convention (tension positive; τxy\tau_{xy} positive as in the notes).
  2. Find θ\theta counter-clockwise from xx to x′x' (clockwise is negative). If the plane is given by the angle of its face, use the angle of its outward normal.
  3. Compute σx+σy2\dfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}, σx−σy2\dfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2} and 2θ2\theta.
  4. σx′\sigma_{x'}, τx′y′\tau_{x'y'} and σy′\sigma_{y'} from the three equations.
  5. Check σx′+σy′=σx+σy\sigma_{x'}+\sigma_{y'}=\sigma_x+\sigma_y.

Example (notes Example A.1, θ=+10∘\theta=+10^{\circ}). σx=56\sigma_x=56, σy=−84\sigma_y=-84, τxy=−42\tau_{xy}=-42 MPa. σx+σy2=−14\dfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}2=-14, σx−σy2=70\dfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}=70, 2θ=20∘2\theta=20^{\circ}.

  1. σx′\sigma_{x'}​=−14+70cos⁡20∘+(−42)sin⁡20∘=-14+70\cos20^{\circ}+(-42)\sin20^{\circ}​=37.4=37.4 MPa.
  2. τx′y′\tau_{x'y'}​=−70sin⁡20∘+(−42)cos⁡20∘=-70\sin20^{\circ}+(-42)\cos20^{\circ}​=−63.4=-63.4 MPa.
  3. σy′\sigma_{y'}​=−14−70cos⁡20∘−(−42)sin⁡20∘=-14-70\cos20^{\circ}-(-42)\sin20^{\circ}​=−65.4=-65.4 MPa. Check: 37.4−65.437.4-65.4​=−28=-28​=σx+σy=\sigma_x+\sigma_y.

M2. Principal stresses and maximum shear (analytical)

Use when: principal stresses, their planes, τmax\tau_{max} or the absolute maximum shear are wanted.

  1. σx+σy2\dfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}2 and RR​=(σx−σy2)2+τxy2=\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}2\right)^2+\tau_{xy}^2}.
  2. σ1,2=σx+σy2±R\sigma_{1,2}=\dfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}\pm R.
  3. tan⁡2θp\tan2\theta_p​=2τxyσx−σy=\dfrac{2\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}; take θp\theta_p for σ1\sigma_1 within 45∘45^{\circ} of the xx axis if σx>σy\sigma_x>\sigma_y; the other plane is 90∘90^{\circ} away (confirm by substituting in σx′\sigma_{x'}).
  4. τmax=R\tau_{max}=R on planes θp±45∘\theta_p\pm45^{\circ}, with σavg\sigma_{avg}.
  5. If σ1\sigma_1 and σ2\sigma_2 have the same sign, the absolute maximum shear is 12max⁡(∣σ1∣,∣σ2∣)\dfrac12\max(|\sigma_1|,|\sigma_2|) (using σ3=0\sigma_3=0).

Example (notes Example A.5). σx=60\sigma_x=60, σy=−20\sigma_y=-20, τxy=30\tau_{xy}=30 MPa.

  1. σx+σy2=20\dfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}2=20; R=402+302=50R=\sqrt{40^2+30^2}=50.
  2. σ1=70\sigma_1=70 MPa, σ2=−30\sigma_2=-30 MPa.
  3. tan⁡2θp\tan2\theta_p​=2(30)80=\dfrac{2(30)}{80}​=0.75=0.75, so θp=18.43∘\theta_p=18.43^{\circ} counter-clockwise for σ1\sigma_1.
  4. τmax=50\tau_{max}=50 MPa on planes at −26.57∘-26.57^{\circ} and 63.43∘63.43^{\circ}, with σ′=20\sigma'=20 MPa.

M3. Mohr's circle

Use when: you want a picture of all planes, a check on M1 and M2, or the answer with a sketch. Analytical or Mohr? Analytical is exact and best for one plane or a program; Mohr's circle shows everything at once and is best for principal values, τmax\tau_{max} and checking; reading graphically is approximate, so compute from the circle's geometry.

  1. Axes σ\sigma (right) and τ\tau (clockwise shear upward).
  2. Plot X=(σx,−τxy)X=(\sigma_x,-\tau_{xy}) and Y=(σy,+τxy)Y=(\sigma_y,+\tau_{xy}); join them; centre C=(σx+σy)/2C=(\sigma_x+\sigma_y)/2 on the σ\sigma axis.
  3. Circle: centre CC, radius R=CXR=CX.
  4. A,BA,B (on the σ\sigma axis): σ1=C+R\sigma_1=C+R, σ2=C−R\sigma_2=C-R. D,ED,E: τmax=R\tau_{max}=R.
  5. Angle XCA=2θpXCA=2\theta_p (measure in the same sense as the element rotation).
  6. For a plane θ\theta from xx: rotate CXCX by 2θ2\theta in the same sense; the new point has coordinates (σx′,−τx′y′)(\sigma_{x'},-\tau_{x'y'}).

Example (notes Example A.6; same data as A.5). X=(60,−30)X=(60,-30), Y=(−20,+30)Y=(-20,+30), C=(20,0)C=(20,0), R=50R=50.

  1. σ1=70\sigma_1=70, σ2=−30\sigma_2=-30 MPa; XCAXCA​=tan⁡−1(30/40)=\tan^{-1}(30/40)​=36.87∘=36.87^{\circ} so θp=18.43∘\theta_p=18.43^{\circ} counter-clockwise.
  2. D=(20,50)D=(20,50): τmax=50\tau_{max}=50 MPa, σavg=20\sigma_{avg}=20 MPa.
  3. Plane at θ=30∘\theta=30^{\circ} counter-clockwise: rotate CXCX by 60∘60^{\circ} to +23.13∘+23.13^{\circ}: σx′\sigma_{x'}​=20+50cos⁡23.13∘=20+50\cos23.13^{\circ}​=66.0=66.0; τx′y′\tau_{x'y'}​=−50sin⁡23.13∘=-50\sin23.13^{\circ}​=−19.6=-19.6; σy′=−26.0\sigma_{y'}=-26.0 MPa.

M4. Thin cylinder and sphere: stresses and design

Use when: a thin shell (t≤r/10t\le r/10) under internal pressure; stresses, allowable pressure or required thickness.

  1. Check t≤r/10t\le r/10.
  2. Cylinder: σh=pD2t\sigma_h=\dfrac{pD}{2t}, σL=pD4t\sigma_L=\dfrac{pD}{4t}; sphere: σ=pD4t\sigma=\dfrac{pD}{4t}.
  3. With joints: divide σh\sigma_h by ηl\eta_l and σL\sigma_L by ηc\eta_c.
  4. Shear: absolute τmax=σh/2\tau_{max}=\sigma_h/2 (cylinder), in-plane (σh−σL)/2(\sigma_h-\sigma_L)/2; sphere σ/2\sigma/2.
  5. Design: set each stress equal to σallow\sigma_{allow} and take the larger thickness; for allowable pressure take the smaller pp.

Example (notes Example B.4, design with joint efficiency). Boiler shell, D=1000D=1000 mm, p=1.5p=1.5 MPa, σallow=100\sigma_{allow}=100 MPa, ηl=0.8\eta_l=0.8, ηc=0.5\eta_c=0.5.

  1. Hoop: tt​=pD2σηl=\dfrac{pD}{2\sigma\eta_l}​=1.5×10002×100×0.8=\dfrac{1.5\times1000}{2\times100\times0.8}​=9.375=9.375 mm.
  2. Longitudinal: tt​=pD4σηc=\dfrac{pD}{4\sigma\eta_c}​=1.5×10004×100×0.5=\dfrac{1.5\times1000}{4\times100\times0.5}​=7.50=7.50 mm.
  3. Adopt the larger: t≈10t\approx10 mm.

M5. Strains and changes in dimensions

Use when: change in diameter, length or volume; or pressure from a strain-gauge reading.

  1. Find σh\sigma_h, σL\sigma_L (M4).
  2. εh=σh−νσLE\varepsilon_h=\dfrac{\sigma_h-\nu\sigma_L}{E}, εL=σL−νσhE\varepsilon_L=\dfrac{\sigma_L-\nu\sigma_h}{E}.
  3. ΔD=εhD\Delta D=\varepsilon_hD, ΔL=εLL\Delta L=\varepsilon_LL, ΔVV=2εh+εL\dfrac{\Delta V}{V}=2\varepsilon_h+\varepsilon_L, V=πD2L4V=\dfrac{\pi D^2L}{4}. Sphere: ε=(1−ν)σE\varepsilon=\dfrac{(1-\nu)\sigma}{E}, ΔVV=3ε\dfrac{\Delta V}V=3\varepsilon.
  4. For a measured strain: invert step 2 to get σh\sigma_h then pp.

Example (notes Example B.2). D=800D=800 mm, t=10t=10 mm, L=2500L=2500 mm, p=2p=2 MPa, E=200E=200 GPa, ν=0.3\nu=0.3.

  1. σh=80\sigma_h=80, σL=40\sigma_L=40 MPa. εh\varepsilon_h​=80−0.3(40)200 000=\dfrac{80-0.3(40)}{200\,000}​=3.4×10−4=3.4\times10^{-4}; εL=8.0×10−5\varepsilon_L=8.0\times10^{-5}.
  2. ΔD=0.272\Delta D=0.272 mm; ΔL=0.200\Delta L=0.200 mm; ΔVV=7.6×10−4\dfrac{\Delta V}V=7.6\times10^{-4}, ΔV=955\Delta V=955 cm3^3.

M6. Thick cylinder (Lamé)

Use when: t>r/10t>r/10; stresses at given radii; design thickness; external pressure; unknown pressure from a stress limit.

  1. Write σr=A−B/r2\sigma_r=A-B/r^2, σθ=A+B/r2\sigma_\theta=A+B/r^2 (tension positive; σr=−p\sigma_r=-p at a loaded surface).

M8. Deflection under a single load from energy

  1. Compute UU for the member (M7).
  2. 12Pδ=U\tfrac12P\delta=U so δ=2U/P\delta=2U/P (for a torque, 12Tφ=U\tfrac12T\varphi=U).

M9. Combined stress states (axial plus torsion; shell plus torque)

Use when: a point carries a normal stress and a torsional shear (shaft with axial force and torque; thin cylinder with pressure and torque).

  1. Find each stress separately: σ=P/A\sigma=P/A, τ=Tr/J\tau=Tr/J (shaft; τ=T/2πr2t\tau=T/2\pi r^2t for a thin tube); σh\sigma_h, σL\sigma_L for the shell.
  2. Assign them to σx\sigma_x, σy\sigma_y, τxy\tau_{xy} with correct signs at the point and surface being examined.
  3. Apply M2 (analytical) or M3 (Mohr) for principal stresses and τmax\tau_{max}.
  4. State the likely failure plane: brittle material cracks perpendicular to σ1\sigma_1; ductile material yields on a plane of τmax\tau_{max}.

Example (notes Example A.7). Cast-iron shaft: axial compression 30 MPa and torsional shear 50 MPa. σx=−30\sigma_x=-30, σy=0\sigma_y=0, τxy=50\tau_{xy}=50.

  1. C=−15C=-15; RR​=152+502=\sqrt{15^2+50^2}​=52.2=52.2 MPa.
  2. σ1=37.2\sigma_1=37.2 MPa; σ2=−67.2\sigma_2=-67.2 MPa; τmax=52.2\tau_{max}=52.2 MPa; θp=53.3∘\theta_p=53.3^{\circ} counter-clockwise for σ1\sigma_1.
  3. Cast iron is weak in tension, so it cracks perpendicular to σ1\sigma_1 (a helix at about 37∘37^{\circ} to the axis).
